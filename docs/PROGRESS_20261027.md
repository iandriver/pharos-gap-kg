# pharos-gap-kg: progress report, 27 October 2026

Bio-Agent Knowledge Graph Construction Challenge, open-source track. Ian Driver, solo entry.
Repository: <https://github.com/iandriver/pharos-gap-kg> (Apache-2.0).
The questions, served over MCP: <https://pm4ie6tprh.us-east-1.awsapprunner.com/mcp>, to add as a
custom connector in Claude (see Try it).

## Summary

`pharos-gap-kg` builds one knowledge graph from the public Pharos 3.19 database and a few other
public sources, and serves it through eight typed questions that a person or a language model can
ask. Its main use is to say what is missing. For a disease, it lists the genes the graph ties to it
and what each lacks: a compound, enough evidence to leave Pharos's least-studied level, a GWAS signal
resolved to one gene, or a genetic association at all. Each gap comes with the evidence that would
close it and, where it was measured, how often such a gap has closed. Where the gap is a missing
genetic association, the answer gives the number of cases a study would need to find a common variant
at a stated odds ratio; where a GWAS signal
sits on several genes, it lists them in the order the graph's other evidence points to.

Every edge names its source, and the graph records the published file it was read from and that
file's digest. A GWAS link names its studies and papers, an expression observation its row in the
release, and a disease source's assertion the source's own evidence where the source gives any. Every
edge the pipeline removes stays in the graph as a typed gap, which names the rule that removed it and
the evidence that would restore it. Every prediction is scored on links reported after the data it was
made from, with three exceptions, stated where they apply: Pharos's undated context, which some
scorers read; the 2026 ontology, which lineage reads, so each result that uses lineage is reported
beside the same model without it; and a ranking asked as of an earlier year, which uses weights fitted
on links to 2022, so what it shows as reported later is an illustration, not a backtest.

Coding agents wrote the pipeline (Claude Code, with Codex as a second reviewer) and I directed them.
Each measurement was defined in `docs/PROTOCOL.md` and committed before it was computed, and every
change made after seeing data is a dated amendment there. The built pipeline is deterministic code.
At question time a model only chooses which question to ask and words the answer. A model that reads
abstracts was tried as a pruning rule and lost to a rule that needs none. One agent stage maps tables
the graph does not yet hold: a model proposes how a Pharos table becomes Biolink edges, and a validator
in code accepts or refuses it. Three Claude models qualified on the nine tables the graph already maps,
and their mappings of three new tables are exported apart, not yet joined.

Where it stands on each criterion and on the challenge's key question:

- **Usability.** Using the typed questions, three small hosted models each got 34 or 35 of 35
  code-graded tasks right, on four separate frozen task sets, and did better than with SQL over the
  same tables on two of the four. A first frozen set, before the faults it showed were fixed, gave
  27, 27 and 26 against SQL's 30, 27 and 30. No researcher outside the project has used it yet; the
  test is written and frozen, and I take it first myself, on a separate set, reported apart.
- **Pruning.** Rules are scored on planted false pairs against a bar stated in advance. A cut on PubMed
  co-mention clears the bar on five of the eleven disease sources tested and is applied: 7,248 of
  324,882 rows are withheld as typed gaps, 6,999 by the cut and 249 by in-graph rules on Monarch. A
  model that reads the abstracts does worse than that cut. Where ClinGen's expert panels have judged
  a kept row, 6,562 rows, they support 86.7% and are against 4.6%. They support the 26 withheld rows
  they have judged less often, though not on UniProt Disease, where they support 10 of 11. The rule
  counts a second source by identifier: 4,081 withheld rows have a row from another source, mostly
  DisGeNET, on a condition of the same name.
- **Explainability.** `why` returns the studies, papers and years behind an edge, down to the digest of
  the file they were read from. Where there is no edge it says what evidence exists nearby, what the
  largest study of the trait could have found, and the study that would close the gap.
- **Standards.** The graph is one KGX export with BioLink categories and predicates, and it passes
  the `kgx-gaps` conformance suite. The reference KGX validator finds no unknown predicate and no
  missing property against Biolink 4.4.3; it flags the identifiers of 1,044 of 59,071 nodes, for four
  stated reasons. It does not check Biolink's own constraints on classes, domains and qualifiers.
  Against those every one of the 640,321 edges passes, after a remodel that put the disease sources'
  assertions on gene nodes; before it, 614,400 of 620,168 edges failed.
- **The key question.** On links reported later, no graph model has beaten a parameter-free neighbour
  score computed from Pharos links alone. Joined into a graph model, Pharos's other content adds to
  that model, including on links neither lineage nor position explains, but not enough to reach the
  neighbour score, and since that content is undated the gain is an upper bound. Three small fitted
  scores that add gene position beat the neighbour score on links first reported from 2023, at loci
  the training links already hold; on the links nothing earlier explains, the one named in advance is
  level with it at best.

## Try it

The questions are served at `https://pm4ie6tprh.us-east-1.awsapprunner.com/mcp`. In Claude, add that
address as a custom connector named pharos-gap-kg (Settings, Connectors) and ask, for example, "Using
pharos-gap-kg, what are the gaps for Parkinson disease?". It is the repository's container image with
the Pharos 3.19 bundle, on AWS App Runner, deployed from a commit by `scripts/deploy_apprunner.sh`.

To run them locally: the release carries the bundle the questions read as a 16 MB archive (20 MB
unpacked), so they can be tried without building anything:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/iandriver/pharos-gap-kg && cd pharos-gap-kg
uv venv && source .venv/bin/activate && uv pip install -e ".[server]" -c constraints.txt
python -m pharos_gap_kg.release get pharos319     # downloads the bundle, checks every digest, unpacks it
python -m pharos_gap_kg.ask gaps "Parkinson disease"
python -m pharos_gap_kg.ask gaps "Parkinson disease" --kind no_genetic_support --top 10
python -m pharos_gap_kg.ask why ACHE "Alzheimer disease"
python -m pharos_gap_kg.ask why APOE "Alzheimer disease" --as-of 2010
python -m pharos_gap_kg.ask predict "type 2 diabetes mellitus" --unexplained-only
python -m pharos_gap_kg.server        # the same questions over MCP
python -m pharos_gap_kg.server --http # over HTTP at /mcp, for a client that connects by URL
python -m pharos_gap_kg.desktop install  # into Claude Desktop on macOS (quit it first); checks the server answers
```

The answer to `why APOE "Alzheimer disease" --as-of 2010` begins:

> APOE is linked to Alzheimer disease because 4 GWAS studies in 4 paper(s), the first in 2007,
> reported a genome-wide significant association assigned to this gene. 25 more studies came after 2010.

It goes on to the other sources that assert the pair, and the JSON beside it names each study, its
PubMed id and year, and the edge id. `docs/ASK.md` shows every question with its answer.

## Terms

- **Mean average precision over traits.** The measure for link prediction: for each trait, how high the
  targets later reported for it rank among its candidates, averaged over traits. Chance is 0.0178.
- **Neighbour score.** Ranks a target for a trait by how similar that trait is to the traits the
  target is already linked to. It has no fitted parameter.
- **Lineage** is a trait's ancestors and descendants in the EFO ontology. **Position** is a gene within
  500,000 bases of a gene that already has the link. A **locus** is such genes taken together, since a
  GWAS signal is assigned to every gene near it.
- **Tdark** is the least-studied of Pharos's four development levels (Tdark, Tbio, Tchem, Tclin). A
  target's **potency cutoff** is the activity Pharos requires of a compound, for the target's protein
  family, before the target counts as having one.
- A **one-source pair** is a target and disease that only one disease source asserts. A **planted
  pair** is a false pair made by crossing two of a source's own pairs, used to score pruning rules.
- **KGX** is the Knowledge Graph Exchange format, nodes and edges in tab-separated files with Biolink
  Model categories and predicates. **MCP** is the Model Context Protocol, through which an AI assistant
  calls the eight questions as tools.
- **LightGCN** is a graph neural network that learns a vector for each target and trait by smoothing
  over the graph. A **setting** is one combination of a model's choices (vector length, steps,
  learning rate, weight on the vectors' length); each graph model was tuned over 44 on the 2018
  split, and the LightGCN retuned for the second test over 8.

## What it finds: gaps, and what would close them

`gaps` with a disease lists the genes the graph ties to it (by a GWAS link, by two or more disease
sources, or as the mechanism target of an indicated drug) and what they lack, in six lists: no drug
or compound, still Tdark, a GWAS signal that sits on several genes, a tie with no GWAS link, rows a
pruning rule withheld, and an expression observation alone. Each list says what would close the gap
and gives a rate where one was measured. An absent GWAS link is reported with the smallest effect the
largest study of the trait could have found, read from the study's size in the GWAS Catalog, and with
the study that would close it: the cases needed to find a common variant at odds ratios of 1.05, 1.1
and 1.2, and for a gene-based test of rare variants. A locus lists its genes in the order of what the
graph holds for them besides the link.

For Parkinson disease the graph ties 345 genes: 248 by a GWAS link, 88 by two or more sources and 24
as the mechanism target of an indicated drug. 251 have no drug and no compound at their family's
cutoff, and 22 are still Tdark. 228 of the 248 GWAS-linked genes sit in 39 loci that carry the link on
more than one gene. 97 genes are tied with no GWAS link; the first listed, ATP13A2 and PARK7, are each
asserted by six sources. The largest study behind the trait's links needed an odds ratio of about
1.08 to find a variant carried by 20% of chromosomes, so their absence says nothing of rarer variants
or smaller effects. At 1.05 a study would need about 55,000 cases at that study's ratio of controls,
and a gene-based test of rare variants about 13,000 cases with sequence data. Of the 33 loci that no
tie singles out, one source or an expression observation points to a gene in 3, among them APOE and
SLC41A1.

Over the 513 disease traits of the release:

- 92.8% of GWAS-linked genes share their locus with another gene that carries the same link, and
  other evidence in the graph singles out one gene in 9.2% of those loci;
- 77.3% of tied genes have no drug and no compound at their family's potency cutoff;
- for the 457 traits whose largest study has cases and controls, the median is an odds ratio of 1.26
  for a variant carried by 20% of chromosomes, and for 270 of them the largest study lacked the cases
  to find a common variant at an odds ratio of 1.2;
- of the 6,395 loci that no tie singles out, one source or an expression observation points to a gene
  in 689 (10.8%).

For one target, `gaps` gives what it lacks for the next Pharos development level. For 5,499 Tdark
targets, Pharos's least-studied level, the gap names the nearest criterion and a distance to it.
Tested against Pharos 4.0.0, where 5,314 of them are found again, that distance ranks the 788 of 5,314
targets that were promoted with a ROC area of 0.859 [0.845, 0.872], which is +0.066 [+0.055, +0.077]
over the PubMed score alone. Of targets one criterion short and nearest to it, 53.6% were promoted,
against 5.9% of Tbio targets at the same distance that fell back to Tdark, which is what Pharos's
pipeline alone moves.

A gap is evidence the graph does not hold, and the answer says so. It is not a finding that the gene
matters in the disease. The study sizes are for one variant at one frequency, and the order of a
locus's genes says where the graph's evidence points, not which gene is causal.

The application proposed a gap atlas for three neurodegenerative diseases. The brief asks for a
graph built from Pharos, so the entry was built on Pharos, and the typed gap is what carried over.

## What is built

The rows are the loop the brief asks an agent to run.

| step | what exists | not done |
|---|---|---|
| Ingest | A registry of public files with pinned digests, a resumable fetcher, and a reader that turns the published MySQL dump into typed tables with no database server. From Pharos 3.19: GWAS links, interactions, pathways, GO terms, compounds, drugs and twelve disease sources. From outside it: the EFO ontology, Ensembl gene coordinates, Pharos 4.0.0 development levels and the GWAS Catalog release of 5 Oct 2026. An agent stage for a table the graph does not hold: a model proposes its Biolink mapping from a card that describes the table, and a validator in code accepts or refuses it (`findings/INGEST.md`). | Joining the three new tables it mapped: OMIM's mapping keeps one phenotype per row and needs a resolver that gives every one. Gene Ontology annotations, which Biolink 4.4.3's domains refuse for genes and proteins. |
| Filter | A client model turns a question in plain language into one of the eight typed questions. A name is resolved against the graph or refused, and every answer is as of a year. The nodes and edges behind any answer come back as KGX with the graph's own ids (`subgraph`). | |
| Clean | Declared pruning rules, each scored on planted false pairs. A withheld row stays as a typed gap. | Three disease sources that no rule reaches. |
| Limit | The questions read a 20 MB bundle of tables. | Nothing further; the graph is small. |
| Maintain | Every input and derived file carries a digest. A traced map of what each build stage reads and writes says which outputs a changed input makes stale, and a rebuild re-derives only those (`findings/MAINTENANCE.md`). | |

The KGX export is one graph of 640,321 edges and 30,484 gaps over 59,071 nodes. Its parts share node
ids, and each is exported as what its source asserts:

- 295,327 GWAS links between 18,005 of Pharos's 20,412 protein targets and 2,247 traits, from 12,335
  studies in 3,141 papers, each dated by its first study;
- 154,363 assertions of a gene with a condition from nine disease sources, each naming the protein
  the source's row was for, and the 7,248 rows a pruning rule withheld, as gaps. 20,153 edges make
  each protein the product of its gene (20,011 genes); 4 assertions whose protein has no gene in the
  release stay on the protein, under `biolink:associated_with`. Pharos's disease table has twelve sources: one, "JensenLab
  Experiment TIGA", repeats the GWAS data and is left out; eleven were tested for pruning; and two of
  those, DrugCentral Indication and Expression Atlas, are exported as below rather than as assertions;
- 159,846 expression observations, each with a direction, a fold change and a p-value, kept apart
  from the assertions;
- 1,782 drugs with 4,295 target and 5,943 indication edges. A DrugCentral indication row is the path
  through a drug, and 10,327 of 13,919 rows run through the target the release marks as the drug's
  mechanism of action;
- 23,236 target-level gaps;
- 394 `biolink:same_as` edges that tie a GWAS trait id the ontology has retired to its current term,
  which is what joins 346 GWAS traits to the conditions the other sources name.

Interactions, pathways, GO terms and compound memberships are not in the export. The scorers and the
graph models read them as tables.

## Status by judging criterion

### Usability (40%)

Eight questions run from the command line or from any MCP client: `find`, `known`, `why`, `connect`,
`predict`, `whats_new`, `gaps` and `status`; `gaps` takes a target or a disease. A name that matches
nothing returns "not in the graph". An absent link is never reported as a negative. `known`, `why`
and `connect` read every part of the graph: beside the dated links they say which sources assert a
pair, which rows are withheld and on what count, and which drugs act on a target and treat the
condition. `connect` answers across two hops, for example the 45 targets that Parkinson disease and
Crohn's disease share. A prediction carries the parts of its score and how often candidates at its
rank were later reported.

Each test put 35 code-graded tasks to three small hosted models (mercury-2.5, deepseek-v4-flash,
glm-5.3-flash, in that order below) under three conditions. Tasks and expected answers were frozen
before any model saw them.

| tasks on | typed questions | SQL over the same tables | no tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| the first seven questions | 35, 35, 35 | 31, 33, 35 | 1, 4, 1 |
| target-level gaps | 34, 35, 35 | 33, 33, 34 | 3, 11, 9 |
| rankings and their rates | 35, 35, 34 | 32, 34, 35 | 0, 0, 0 |
| other sources, drugs and the gaps of a disease | 34, 35, 35 | 28, 34, 35 | 0, 0, 1 |

No model answered about an invented gene, drug or disease as if it were known. On the share of tasks
done correctly, the typed questions beat SQL on the first set (+0.057 [+0.010, +0.105]) and on the
fourth (+0.067 [+0.019, +0.124]), and are not shown to on the other two (+0.038 [+0.000, +0.086] and
+0.029 [-0.019, +0.086]). On the fourth set six of the seven answers that differ are one model's, and
the result would not hold without that model. In that set every typed answer a model gave was right;
the one miss is a question a provider refused to answer. Each set followed a development round, and
the faults that round found, in the test or in the tools, were fixed before the fresh set was
written. The first round was itself a frozen test, graded as stated: typed 27, 27 and 26 against SQL
30, 27 and 30, so neither verdict was met; it became the development round of the first row. All
rounds are in `findings/USABILITY.md`.

Setting the questions up in Claude Desktop is one command, `desktop install`, which installs a copy

### Accuracy and pruning (30%)

A pruning rule is scored by planting false pairs in a source and counting how many it withholds, and
how many real pairs it withholds with them. The bar, stated before any rule ran: at least 80% of
planted pairs withheld, at most 50% of real pairs that only one source asserts.

| rule | sources that pass, of 11 |
|---|---|
| in-graph corroboration: another source, a related disease or an interaction partner has the pair | 1 |
| no PubMed abstract mentions the gene with the disease | 2 |
| a cut on PubMed lift: co-mention against chance, cut set on half the sample and scored on the other | 5 |
| a model reads the abstracts and must quote the passage it relies on | 3 |

The in-graph rule withheld 38.9% of 16,250 planted pairs in the first round, and 32.7% of 12,431 when
measured again on a planting that corrected a fault in the first (`findings/PRUNING2.md`). Its failing criterion is "an interaction
partner has the same disease", which holds for most random pairs. At the same cost in real pairs the
model reader withholds 81.1% of planted pairs and the lift cut 87.8% (-0.067 [-0.103, -0.037]), so
the rule that needs no model is the one applied. No source's own confidence field orders its pairs
the way the literature does.

The cut is applied to the five sources it passes on, and on Monarch the in-graph rule with it. Of the
7,248 rows withheld, 5,982 are CTD's. The 6,999 the cut withholds each carry the papers counted, the
lift and the cut; the 249 withheld by in-graph rules alone carry the rule and what would restore the row.

Limits: the lift cut was added after the first counts were seen, and the record marks it so. CTD's
cost over all its one-source pairs is 46.5% against the bar of 50%, above the 35.0% [26.4%, 44.7%] it
cost on the pairs it was scored on; Monarch's falls outside its interval too. The rule counts a
second source by the condition's
identifier: 4,081 of the 7,248 withheld rows have a kept row from another source on a condition of the
same name under another identifier, 4,059 of them DisGeNET's, whose curated set draws on CTD. Whether
these count as second sources is open (`docs/data/withheld_named_elsewhere_pharos319.json`). Its pass first rested on 100
planted pairs; on 400 more, drawn with them and searched only after the check was written down, the
cut withholds 82.2% [78.2%, 85.7%] of planted pairs, so it stands. Expression Atlas, DrugCentral
Indication and AmyCo are out of reach of every rule tried, full text included: on DrugCentral
Indication's first 100 planted and real pairs it withholds 76.0% and 53.0%, and on AmyCo's first 50,
80.0% and 70.0%. The first two are not assertions of a gene with a disease, and the graph exports
them as what they are, a path through a drug and an observation of expression. That is a change of
form and not a pruning result.

Against expert curation (`findings/CLINGEN.md`, registered before it was run): ClinGen's panels have
judged gene-disease validity for 3,066 genes, mostly Mendelian. Where they have judged a kept row,
6,562 rows, they support 86.7% [85.8%, 87.5%] and are against 4.6% [4.1%, 5.1%] (disputed, refuted or
no known relationship), from 1.4% on UniProt Disease to 11.8% on JensenLab Knowledge UniProtKB-KW.
They have judged 26 withheld rows. Pooled over the five pruned sources, the share they support is
higher among kept rows than withheld ones by 0.275 [0.127, 0.425], and by 0.222 [0.082, 0.356] without
Monarch, whose sources may include ClinGen. On UniProt Disease, the one source with enough judged
withheld rows for a verdict of its own, no difference is detected: ClinGen supports 10 of the 11 rows
withheld from it. Of the 15 withheld rows ClinGen supports, the cut withheld 13 because the PubMed
search found no paper naming the gene with the disease, most of them Mendelian subtypes. Of the 141
kept rows of the pruned sources that ClinGen is against, 126 are asserted by a second source, so the
cut never tested them. 109,749 of the 161,611 rows are outside ClinGen's scope, and there the error
rate among the rows that are kept is not measured.

Link prediction is under "The key question" below.

### Explainability (20%)

An edge id is a digest of what the edge claims. `why APOE "Alzheimer disease" --as-of 2010` returns
the four GWAS studies behind that edge with their PubMed ids and years, the first in 2007, and says
25 more came later. `graph lineage` follows the same edge to the SHA-256 of the Pharos file it was
read from.

Where there is no edge, `why` says that no study in this source had reported one by that year, that
this is not evidence of no association, and what exists nearby: links to related traits, links at
neighbouring genes, and the target's rank among the trait's candidates.

A withheld row is explained the same way. For PTGER1 and hydronephrosis, `why` says that one CTD row
is withheld because PubMed mentions the gene with the disease no more often than the cut for its
source: no paper mentions both, a lift of 0 against a cut of 2.63, and a lift above the cut or a second
source would restore it.

A candidate comes with a rate measured on later links. Of the ten highest candidates for a trait,
8.9% were reported in 2023 to 2026, against 0.49% of all candidates. Where no earlier link at the
locus or under a related trait explains the candidate, the rate is 4.0%.

The nodes and edges an answer rests on come back as KGX, from the command line or as a ninth MCP
tool, `subgraph`: every edge and gap the answer cites, every edge and gap between two nodes it names,
and their end nodes. They are built from the bundle, and each of the 639,927 edges and 30,484 gaps the
bundle can give equals the export's row in id, subject, predicate, object, category, knowledge source,
knowledge level and agent type (`docs/ASK.md`).

### Standards (10%)

Nodes and edges are KGX. Nodes use eight BioLink categories (`biolink:Protein`, `biolink:Gene`,
`biolink:Disease`, `biolink:DiseaseOrPhenotypicFeature`, `biolink:Drug`, `biolink:PhenotypicFeature`,
`biolink:BiologicalProcess`, `biolink:PhenotypicQuality`) and edges eight BioLink predicates
(`biolink:genetically_associated_with`, `biolink:gene_associated_with_condition`,
`biolink:correlated_with`, `biolink:has_gene_product`, `biolink:applied_to_treat`,
`biolink:directly_physically_interacts_with`, `biolink:same_as`, `biolink:associated_with`). An
expression edge carries the aspect `expression` as a qualifier, and its direction and effect size as
properties, since no Biolink 4.4.3 gene-to-disease class carries a subject direction. Each edge names
its primary knowledge source, its knowledge level and its agent type, and an edge read through Pharos
names Pharos as its aggregator. The level and the agent type are `not_provided` on 105,344 assertions,
from sources that mix curated and mined rows without saying which a row is, and the agent type on the
20,153 gene edges. The merged graph passes 10 of 10 checks of the `kgx-gaps` conformance suite, with every input
row an edge or a gap. `kgx-gaps` is this entry's own suite, and the gaps file is its extension, not a
KGX file. One predicate is outside BioLink, `pharosgapkg:evidence_missing_for` on gaps, with a
registered rationale, and 979 indications the release names without an identifier are nodes under the
local prefix, flagged as not grounded.

Of the 1,404 traits with a link by 2018, 505 carry an ontology id that has since been retired and
replaced. Lineage is computed on the live terms and the graph carries the ids as published.

The reference KGX validator (kgx 2.7.0, Biolink 4.4.3, offline) read all 59,071 nodes and 640,321
edges and found no missing required property and no unknown predicate; the one property of the wrong
type it reports is the id of a node kgx-gaps adds for a kind of gap. It
flags the identifiers of 1,044 nodes: 979 indications under the local prefix, 39 traits and
conditions written `Orphanet:` where Biolink writes `orphanet:` (kept, since 30 are the frozen split's
trait ids), 23 under AmyCo's own prefix, and the three nodes kgx-gaps adds for kinds of gap, which
kgx reports once (`findings/STANDARDS.md`). It does not check qualifier values. The export declares no
Biolink version; 4.4.3 is that of the installed package.

The validator built for the ingest stage checks what kgx does not: an edge's association class, a
predicate's domain and range, the class's own constraints and its qualifiers (`findings/INGEST.md`).
Against those, 5,768 of the 620,168 edges passed on 19 Oct. The 294,248 GWAS links and the 5,943 drug
indications used association classes Biolink 4.4.3 does not have, and 4.4.3 makes `biolink:treats` a
mixin; the 154,363 disease-source assertions used `biolink:gene_associated_with_condition`, whose
domain is a gene, with a protein as the subject; and the 159,846 expression observations carried a
subject direction qualifier their class does not carry. Run on the graph's own mappings as a control,
the same validator accepted 1 of the 9 that were scored; the three qualifying models' proposals for
the same tables were accepted on 8 or 9.

The graph was remodelled the same day (`docs/PROTOCOL.md`, amendments of 19 Oct): the assertions run
from gene nodes, the indications are `biolink:applied_to_treat`, and the GWAS links and expression
observations take classes 4.4.3 has. All 640,321 edges now pass, as do the 317,634 of the disease
sources' own graph and the 99,435 of the dated graph of 2018 (`findings/STANDARDS.md`). The graph's own
mappings now pass the validator on 5 of the 9 tables; the other four fail on identifiers as the release
gives them. The census of disease gaps, every table the questions read and fourteen typed answers
compared before and after are what they were, apart from the ids of the 161,611 assertion rows and
5,943 indication edges that moved (`docs/data/remodel_check_pharos319.json`).

Not done: a review by someone who works with BioLink of whether each predicate is the most specific
that fits.

## The key question: does the built graph predict held-out links better

Links are dated by the first GWAS study behind them. Target and ligand pairs can be dated too, by the
papers they cite, but after a 2018 cutoff 44,817 of their 45,491 new pairs bring a compound the graph
did not hold (`findings/SNAPSHOTS.md`), so GWAS links are the dated links a graph can predict. There
are two tests. The first trains on the 99,435 links reported by 2018 and scores 130,369 later links
over 540 traits; 65,523 more later links have a trait or a target with no earlier link and are scored
separately, as cold start (`findings/COLDSTART.md`). There, for a target new to the graph, ranking by
gene position beats ranking by locus degree in all three periods tested, both the new targets for a

No graph model beats the neighbour score on either test. Each kind of Pharos context (interactions,
pathways, GO terms, shared compounds, disease sources) scores 0.019 to 0.032 alone and lowers the
neighbour score when blended with it.

The two graph models declared to take the enriched graph as input, one with the withheld disease
rows and one without, were tuned on all 44 settings and scored once on 16 Oct. With interactions,
pathways, GO terms, compounds and the disease sources joined on, LightGCN scores 0.0866 against 0.0825
without them, +0.0041 [+0.0006, +0.0085], and +0.0033 [+0.0017, +0.0051] on links neither lineage nor
position explains. It stays below the neighbour score, -0.0103 [-0.0194, -0.0023]. Leaving out the
pruned rows changes it by -0.0002 [-0.0007, +0.0002]: pruning does not change prediction. The context
is undated, as of 2022, so the gain it gives is an upper bound on what context known in 2018 would.

The gains over the neighbour score come from gene position. Three combiners of neighbours, degree
and position beat it on the second test. The one the question layer ranks by was the only one of eight
candidates, tried in 28 variants, to beat neighbours when the whole procedure was rehearsed inside the
training years (+0.0038 [+0.0008, +0.0077]), and it was named for the second test before any scorer
read those links. The graph models refitted with the settings frozen on the 2018 split fall well below
neighbours on the later links; retuned, LightGCN is level overall and ahead when counted by locus (+0.0094 [+0.0050, +0.0139]).

The gain is at loci the training data already holds. 35.5% of the 194,017 test links follow from an
earlier link at the same locus or under a related trait. On the rest the served scorer is level with
neighbours once every candidate an earlier link explains is set aside (+0.0005 [-0.0006, +0.0012]),
and behind when only the explained links are (-0.0060 [-0.0081, -0.0043]), a reading the protocol
calls a lower bound for a scorer that uses position. Part of the gain is how GWAS assigns genes: a test
link joins a trait to every gene within 100,000 bases of an associated variant, and position counts
the genes within 500,000 bases that already have the link. That link rule replaced the four declared
rules after the best of them recovered 16.3% of Pharos's links on the studies Pharos and the GWAS
Catalog share; it was chosen by a look outside the committed code and fixed before any later study was
read (`docs/PROTOCOL.md`, 10 Oct). The question layer ranks by the served scorer and says so.

## Findings about the data that other teams may need

- The April 2023 (3.16) and April 2024 (3.19) Pharos databases hold identical link tables. 70 of the
  89 tables they share are identical, among them GWAS, disease, interaction, pathway and ligand
  activity. A split by release date has no later links to score.
- One source in the Pharos `disease` table, "JensenLab Experiment TIGA", is the GWAS data under
  another name. Used as context for held-out GWAS links it scores 0.60 where the neighbour score
  reaches 0.10, by reading the answers. A test on GWAS links has to leave it out of the context.
- A fifth of the later GWAS links (26,346 of 130,369) follow from an earlier link at the same locus
  or under a related trait, and the 130,369 links are 31,885 loci. Counted once per locus, target
  degree falls from four times chance to twice.
